    How it was built. Site, lot boundary, pool, garage and neighbours are placed from the NSW Spatial Services aerial and cadastre (Lot A DP389934, 878 m²). House footprints come from the listing floor plan (marked not to scale, read at about 25 px per metre and checked against the room sizes printed on it). Elevations and materials come from the 2015 and 2021 listing photos. Levels: studio floor 0 m, main floor +2.9 m, upper floor +5.8 m, street about −5.5 m. Expect ±0.5 m on any dimension. A concept sketch for talking to builders, not a survey.
    
    
    Ground levels checked against NSW elevation data. Homer St footpath is about 17.5 m AHD, so the studio floor is about 23 m AHD (the Camera panel shows AHD for any view). The block falls about 21 m from the back street (about 38.5 m AHD) to Homer St over 69 m, steepest in the front yard at about 1 in 2, facing north-north-east. The house and front yard match the model within about 1–1.5 m. The back half does not: the model has the garage and back street about 15 m above Homer St, NSW data says about 21 m. That data is only good to a few metres and the back terraces come from photos, so the back is left as drawn until a survey (already in the approvals cost). Terrain blocks no sun: the land falls away from north-west through north to north-east, and the land horizon is under 4° wherever the sun rises or sets (east-north-east in winter, east-south-east in summer), so only neighbours and trees cast shade.
    
    
    Big balcony. Covers the whole living-room roof plus the extension roof, about 25 m², opening off all three bedrooms. The roof needs strengthening and waterproofing, and a street-facing balcony may raise privacy questions with council.
    
    
    Juliet balconies. Each bedroom window becomes full-height doors with a metal rail just outside, so the doors open wide for air and the view but nobody steps out. The roof below stays a plain roof. The cost per balcony is my own estimate (doors $3.5k–7k, rail $1.5k–3k, flashing $0.5k–1k).
